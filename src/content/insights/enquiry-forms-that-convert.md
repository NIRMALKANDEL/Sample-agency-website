You can do everything right on social and still lose the lead at the last step. The enquiry form is where intent is highest and patience is lowest. An analysis of more than 300 demo and enquiry pages by [GrowthSpree](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/b2b-saas-demo-page-conversion-killers-300-companies-analyzed) found the same problems again and again.

## 1. Too many fields

Typical forms ask for 8–12 fields. The analysis estimates that every field beyond the core five cuts conversions by roughly 10–15%.

**Fix:** ask for name, email, company, what they need and a short message. Turn qualifiers like budget into quick picks rather than typing, and collect everything else on the call.

## 2. No proof where people decide

The form page is often the only page without trust signals.

**Fix:** put a short line of proof next to the form, such as a recognisable client, a specific result you’re allowed to share, or a real testimonial.

## 3. Booking on a different page

Sending someone from a form to a separate scheduling page reportedly loses 30–50% of them before they book.

**Fix:** embed the calendar. On our own site, when you send a brief, a calendar appears immediately with your details already filled in.

## 4. No idea what happens next

People hesitate when they don’t know what they’re signing up for.

**Fix:** one line under the button: who replies, how fast, and what the call covers.

## 5. Forms that fight phones

Long forms on small screens are where most abandonment happens.

**Fix:** use large tap targets and the right keyboard for each field (email, phone). Offer a one-tap alternative like WhatsApp or a direct call.

## A quick audit you can run today

- Count your fields. More than seven? Cut.
- Fill in your form on your phone, one-handed.
- Submit it. Does the confirmation tell you what happens next?
- Time how long until someone replies.

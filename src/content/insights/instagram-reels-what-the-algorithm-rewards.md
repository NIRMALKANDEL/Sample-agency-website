If your Reels used to reach new people and now mostly reach your followers, the rules changed under you. Instagram has been clear about the direction: reward content people **share privately**, **watch to the end**, and that is **original**. Here is what that means in practice.

## Sends are the signal that matters most

According to [SocialPilot’s 2026 breakdown](https://www.socialpilot.co/de/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm), DM sends are weighted roughly three to five times more than likes when Instagram decides whether to show a Reel to non-followers. A hundred sends can do more than a thousand likes.

That changes the brief for every piece of content. Instead of asking “will people like this?”, ask **“who would someone send this to?”** A good Reel has a recipient in mind: the friend who always cooks on Sundays, the co-founder who hates spreadsheets, the sister planning a wedding.

**Try this:** end your Reel on a line that names the recipient. “Send this to whoever handles your company’s books” is a prompt, not engagement bait.

## Completion beats length

Watch time and completion rate are the next strongest signals. A 90-second Reel that people finish can outperform a three-minute Reel they leave halfway through. The same source reports that although Reels can be up to 20 minutes long, recommendations to non-followers effectively stop around three minutes.

- Hook in the first three seconds, with motion or on-screen text inside the first half-second.
- Cut every pause you can. Trim until it hurts, then trim again.
- Keep discovery content under three minutes. Save longer pieces for your existing audience.

## Originality is now enforced, everywhere

Content that reuses most of another creator’s visuals or audio is flagged and loses reach to non-followers, sometimes for weeks. Since April 2026, this also applies to photos and carousels. Watermarked videos from other apps are demoted.

If you repurpose TikToks, export them clean. If you react to someone else’s video, add enough of your own footage and commentary that the result is genuinely new.

## Test on strangers with Trial Reels

Accounts with more than 1,000 followers can publish **Trial Reels**, which are shown to non-followers first. If a Trial Reel performs well, you can share it with your followers. If not, your main audience never sees the miss.

We use Trial Reels as a cheap A/B test: three hooks for the same idea, one winner promoted to the grid and, often, into paid ads.

## Captions are search queries now

Instagram’s topic controls let people choose what they want to see more of. Combined with search, that makes **keyword-rich captions** more useful than a block of hashtags. Describe what the Reel is about in plain words people would type.

## A checklist for your next shoot

1. Who will this be sent to? Write their name on the brief.
2. What happens in the first second?
3. Can we cut it to under 60 seconds without losing the point?
4. Is every frame and sound ours?
5. Which three hooks will we test as Trial Reels?
6. Does the caption read like a search query?
